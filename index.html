Travel guide

The practical stuff. Rules change, so double-check anything official before you book.

Visas

Each person sorts their own. What you need depends on your passport, and China’s visa-free rules have changed a lot recently.

  • Check the Chinese embassy or consulate website for the country of your passport
  • Your passport should be valid for at least six months after the trip
  • If you need a visa, apply a couple of months ahead

Travel insurance

  • Get a policy that covers medical care abroad and trip cancellation
  • Check it covers activities like cable cars and mountain walks

Phones and internet

Google, WhatsApp, Instagram and most Western apps don’t work on Chinese Wi-Fi or local SIMs.

  • Buy a travel eSIM before you go. Most route your data outside China, so WhatsApp and Google keep working
  • Install the eSIM at home, switch it on when you land
  • Save this page offline (use the button at the bottom) in case of a bad connection

Money and apps

China runs on phone payments. Cash works but many places prefer QR codes.

  • Alipay and WeChat: both let you link a foreign card. Set them up at home and test with a small payment
  • DiDi: taxis, like Uber (also inside Alipay)
  • Trip.com: trains and attraction tickets in English
  • Amap or Apple Maps: Google Maps is unreliable in China
  • Carry some yuan in cash for small shops

Always carry your passport

You’ll need the actual passport (not a photo) for hotels, trains, flights and most big sights, since tickets are booked in your name.

What to pack

  • Comfortable walking shoes, there’s a lot of walking and some steps
  • Light layers and a light rain jacket, Zhangjiajie can be misty and wet
  • Something warmer for the mountains
  • Plug adapter (China mostly uses Type A and Type I sockets)
  • Tissues and hand sanitiser, many public toilets have none
  • Any regular medication, in original packaging
